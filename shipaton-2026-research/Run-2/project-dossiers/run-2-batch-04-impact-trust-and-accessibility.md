# Run 2 batch 4: impact, trust, and accessibility

This batch examines one 2024 Design winner and all three 2025 Peace Prize
winners. The shared lens is whether a high-stakes promise becomes a credible,
observable mechanism. Controls are same-year products with the closest public
platform, audience, or care-loop evidence available. Non-winner category
selection remains unknown. Analyst scores are not judges' actual scores.

## Rakun Talk

**Winner and match.** Rakun Talk placed second in the 2024 RevenueCat Design
Award. Breathe Zen is the control: both are 2024 native SwiftUI apps with
RevenueCat, a focused wellness or accessibility loop, multimodal visual
feedback, and informal real-user testing. Both shipped on iOS.

### 1. Track interpretation

The 2024 Design Award sought innovative ideas and/or beautiful design and
animation, separate from business viability. Rakun interpreted design as an
interaction that transfers agency: one person selects or creates a request,
the other reads and taps a simple positive or negative response, and optional
translation bridges language. It solved the track narrowly through interaction
design rather than surface ornament.

Breathe Zen interpreted design as a calm, customizable paced-breathing session,
before-and-after mood capture, and progress. It is coherent and polished, but
its expanding bubble is a familiar pattern.

### 2. Product thesis

Rakun prioritizes non-verbal people and people with auditory-processing
challenges, especially while traveling. The trigger is a need that must be
communicated in an unfamiliar, stressful, or multilingual situation. Printed
communication cards are the workaround; they cannot easily cover an unexpected
need or support a response. The wedge is an interactive two-sided card that can
be customized and translated.

Breathe Zen targets people who want a simple paced-breathing habit. The
workaround is an unstructured timer or another wellness app. Its wedge is one-
tap breathing plus mood comparison and visual customization. The founder's
doctor cousin supplied domain inspiration, not clinical validation.

### 3. Core loop

Rakun: a need occurs → choose or create a card → translate if necessary → show
the other person → receive a yes/no response → complete the real-world task →
reuse or adapt the card. The value moment is external and immediate: successful
mutual understanding.

Breathe Zen: stress or routine trigger → start a paced session → follow the
animated bubble → record mood after → see progress → return. The premium limit
creates a monetization moment between repeated sessions.

### 4. Journey

Rakun's event materials show predefined and custom cards, response mode, and
translation. The builder reports successful coffee and quiet-table requests
within seconds. The evidence does not cover onboarding, card search, edit
recovery, offline translation, paywall, restore, or what happens when the other
person does not understand the interface.

Breathe Zen starts with Start Breathing, supports custom sessions, captures
before/after mood, celebrates completion, and shows progress. Its event paywall
offers an $8.99 lifetime unlock, restore, terms, and privacy links. The free
version limits sessions with an eight-hour interval, which can block need-based
use.

### 5. UI and interaction

Rakun's inspected asset uses huge sentence fragments in white cards, high-
contrast black outlines, rounded option chips, and a friendly raccoon. Predicted
attention: “Get what you need” → “Even when you…” → the three constraint chips
→ mascot. In the working flow, the request itself is the primary object and
binary response reduces decision density. Large targets suit tremor, stress,
and shared-device use; switch control, VoiceOver, and alternative response
methods are not evidenced.

Breathe Zen's inspected paywall uses a multicolor breathing visualization,
large benefit headline, feature checks, health claims, and a persistent yellow
Continue button. Predicted attention: glowing bubbles → headline → yellow CTA
→ price → feature list. The CTA is thumb-reachable, but the long benefits panel
and close button at top left add scan distance.

### 6. Visual system

Rakun uses sky blue, white, black, simple geometry, and a custom hooded-raccoon
mascot. The visual language is child-friendly without making the actual need
hard to read. Large black text and plain response affordances support the
communication job.

Breathe Zen uses a dark neutral base, overlapping colored glow circles, violet
product accents, green checks, and a yellow purchase action. The palette evokes
breathing and calm, but several health-benefit colors compete inside the
paywall. Both apps are distinctive; Rakun's system is more semantically tied to
the shared communication task.

### 7. UX quality

Rakun appears fast to first value and can be essential in a pharmacy or food
setting. Its biggest risks are card ambiguity, translation error, inadvertent
disclosure, and reliance on networked DeepL. The team explicitly planned more
research with people who have varied communication needs, meaning broad
accessibility was not yet proven. There is no visible dark pattern.

Breathe Zen offers a familiar low-cognitive-load loop and informal holiday
testing. It does not establish medical outcomes. Limiting free breathing to one
session per eight hours may conflict with an anxiety-driven use case. Mood data
storage, accessibility, crisis boundaries, and failure states are unknown.

### 8. Monetization

Rakun used RevenueCat, but price, free card allowance, trial, and paid boundary
are unknown. Gating basic communication could be ethically difficult; paid
customization, translation volume, or travel packs may be more defensible.

Breathe Zen's event paywall clearly offers unlimited sessions and more
customization for $8.99 lifetime. The payment model is transparent, but its
free-use cooldown gates the core regulation action rather than an enhancement.

### 9. Growth and retention

Rakun can grow through travel and neurodivergent communities, caregiver or
clinician recommendations, and repeated use of saved cards. No ASO, social,
notification, adoption, or retention evidence is available. Translation makes
the addressable audience larger without changing the core loop.

Breathe Zen mentions early downloads without a count and plans RevenueCat
offer experiments. Mood history and breathing habits support retention, but no
behavioral or outcome metric is supplied.

### 10. Technical and operational shape

Rakun uses SwiftUI, DeepL, and RevenueCat. The small stack fits a solo-friendly
product, but translation adds network, cost, language-coverage, latency, and
privacy dependencies. The switch from Apple Translate preserved older-iOS
support at the cost of offline operation. Android was correctly deferred.

Breathe Zen uses SwiftUI, Core Data, and RevenueCat. Replacing unstable
SwiftData near deadline was a practical reliability trade-off. It can plausibly
operate offline. Health-claim copy, local-data migration, and accurate timing
are the main release risks.

### 11. Submission craft

Rakun demonstrates the outcome with a real coffee-order video rather than only
describing cards. The screenshot story moves from promise to request,
translation, and response. Its real-world test is builder-observed, not a
usability study, but judges can understand the mechanism in seconds.

Breathe Zen provides a broad screenshot sequence—session, customization,
overview, mood, programs, education, progress, and paywall—and candidly
documents data-layer replacement. It proves polish but not an unusual design
mechanism or health result.

### 12. Rubric mapping

| Historical criterion | Rakun evidence | Counterevidence | Analyst score | Control score | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Innovative ideas | Flexible, translated, two-sided communication cards | Communication cards pre-exist; broader user research pending | 5 | 4 | High/medium |
| Aesthetics and delight | Cohesive mascot, huge readable cards, simple response model | Motion, haptics, and accessibility settings not evidenced | 5 | 4 | High/medium |

### What the evidence supports

Rakun's visible design value is the reduction of a shared social interaction to
a readable request and an obvious response—not merely the mascot or palette.
Breathe Zen confirms that polish and a coherent wellness loop existed outside
the winners.

### What remains unknown

Translation accuracy, offline recovery, real-user sample size, accessibility
testing, store conversion, and monetization are unknown. Breathe Zen's health
effects and retention are also unmeasured.

### What may transfer to 2026

For accessibility products, show the end-to-end exchange with the intended user
and communication partner. A screen alone cannot establish that both people
understand what to do.

## Heartbeat Hero

**Winner and match.** Heartbeat Hero won the 2025 Peace Prize. LockedIn is the
control: both claim social benefit for young people, use a working mobile
product plus real-time feedback, include a free/paid system, and disclose
serious technical effort. LockedIn is React Native across mobile while
Heartbeat Hero is native iOS/iPadOS; both can reach iOS, but this is a medium-
strength platform match.

### 1. Track interpretation

The Peace criteria were Impact and Feasibility. Heartbeat Hero interpreted
impact as a specific behavioral capability: learn CPR, feel rhythm and depth,
rehearse the emergency call, and locate an AED without buying a manikin. It
constrained the claim with “not a substitute for certified training” and made
core learning free. It reframed a general education app as low-cost physical
practice equipment.

LockedIn interprets impact as a focus movement for young people in developing
countries, implemented through mentor video rooms, matchmaking, streaks, and
rewards. Its aspiration is large, but the relationship between a session and a
measured life outcome is less testable.

### 2. Product thesis

Heartbeat Hero prioritizes students and ordinary bystanders who want to help
during cardiac arrest but lack practice, equipment, or confidence. The trigger
can be proactive learning or a stressful emergency. Workarounds include text
guides, courses, videos, and manikins. The wedge is phone-sensor depth feedback
plus multimodal practice. The founder insight came from a firefighter uncle's
real rescue.

LockedIn prioritizes ambitious young people facing distraction, weak
accountability, and scarce mentorship. Its workaround is solitary focus tools.
The wedge is mentor-led “War Room” sessions and peer matchmaking. The founder
story is credible, but the prioritized segment remains broad.

### 3. Core loop

Heartbeat Hero: choose Learn, Rhythm, Depth, or Call rehearsal → follow a
guided scenario → receive live haptic/audio/visual or AI feedback → review weak
areas → repeat or share completion. In an emergency, the home screen surfaces
address, what3words, GPS, and call action. The AED map is a separate discovery
loop.

LockedIn: join or schedule a focus session → work with mentor or peers → earn a
streak/reward → return and match again. Premium hosting monetizes a higher-
status session type. The causal link from session to education/employment
outcome is assumed rather than measured.

### 4. Journey

Heartbeat Hero's demo shows location first, learning mode choice, standard or
Focus presentation, knowledge checks, results/share, rhythm calibration, depth
scenario and calibration, emergency-call rehearsal, AI review, and AED link.
Most training works offline; the AED map currently opens a website. It includes
free Learn/AED access and verified-student full access. Purchase restore,
sensor failure recovery, and real-emergency mode separation are not fully shown.

LockedIn exposes instant, scheduled, and premium sessions, then video, notes,
music, notifications, and matchmaking. The inspected modal gives three clear
routes, but Jitsi-on-web bridged through WebSockets increases loading and
failure paths. Safety, moderation, missed-session recovery, and cancellation
are not documented.

### 5. UI and interaction

Heartbeat Hero's inspected store asset uses coral, a simple dimensional heart
icon, and huge black text: “Easy, Accessible, CPR Training.” The working demo
uses large cards, standard/focus modes, narration, auto-progress, calibration
voice prompts, and immediate “deeper/perfect” feedback. Predicted attention in
training begins on the mode card, moves to the central instruction, then the
primary Begin/Next action; live feedback becomes dominant during practice.
Turning the phone camera-up on a cushion is unusual and requires the tutorial.

LockedIn's modal puts Instant Session, Schedule Session, and Premium Session in
a white bottom sheet over a muted dashboard. Predicted attention: bold Instant
Session → scheduled option → “Most Popular” badge and green Upgrade. The
premium row has extra color and annotation, increasing commercial salience
before proof of outcome.

### 6. Visual system

Heartbeat Hero uses coral/red for urgency and warmth, black or dark green type,
large rounded cards, soft dimensional icons, and highly legible instructional
layouts. The icon links heart and learning. Multimodal feedback is functional,
not decorative.

LockedIn uses white/gray surfaces, bold dark headings, emoji category icons,
green upgrade accents, and a small yellow popularity badge. It is serviceable
but less distinctive and less tightly linked to the social mission.

### 7. UX quality

Heartbeat Hero shows strong accessibility: VoiceOver, Dynamic Type, haptics,
torch pulses, narration, Focus mode, small text segments, and auto-progress.
Offline-first design improves reliability and privacy. The highest risk is the
builder's unvalidated “millimeter” depth claim; different devices, cushions,
camera coverage, movement, age groups, and CPR surfaces may alter accuracy.
The app appropriately disclaims certified-training replacement.

LockedIn has a credible access mission but depends on live video, mentors,
backend queues, power, network, moderation, and matching. It describes load and
retry architecture, not safeguarding, accessibility, harassment recovery, or
verified mentor quality. Rewards can motivate but may also crowd out intrinsic
focus.

### 8. Monetization

Heartbeat Hero keeps Learn and AED discovery free and grants verified students
lifetime full access. Other premium packaging is not visible. That allocation
protects public benefit while charging for advanced practice or other users,
though verification operations may add support cost.

LockedIn uses RevenueCat and labels paid hosting as LockedIn Plus+. The model
could compensate mentors or support infrastructure, but price, payout,
eligibility, free limits, and whether vulnerable users face exclusion are
unknown.

### 9. Growth and retention

Heartbeat Hero reports training more than 100 students and plans partnerships
with schools, charities, foundations, and training providers. Completion
sharing and repeated practice can support distribution and retention. The
training count is builder-reported; knowledge retention, skill transfer, and
rescue outcomes are not measured.

LockedIn targets a million “young dreamers” and plans accelerators, testing,
and feedback. It supplies no active-user, completed-session, mentor, retention,
or outcome measure.

### 10. Technical and operational shape

Heartbeat Hero uses Swift/SwiftUI, ARKit, 200 Hz IMU, adaptive Kalman filtering,
AVAudioEngine, haptics, audio, torch, on-device Apple Intelligence, RevenueCat,
and a limited Django/PostgreSQL/Railway surface. Feature-rich and fallback
tracking modes improve coverage. iPhone model requirements affect AI features;
sensor validation and OS changes are substantial release risks.

LockedIn uses Expo/React Native, FastAPI, RabbitMQ, WebSockets, Jitsi web,
OneSignal, RevenueCat, and custom hosting. It is more operationally distributed
than its core focus loop requires. Live communications and event-driven seams
increase support, security, cost, and abuse-handling burden.

### 11. Submission craft

Heartbeat Hero's three-minute overview plus extended demo makes the key impact
mechanism visible: clap feedback, phone-on-cushion depth calibration, and an
emergency-call conversation. It also shows accessibility settings, free access,
offline behavior, results, and location. The story distinguishes the unshipped
2024 prototype from the 2025 public rebuild.

LockedIn's Devpost story is emotionally compelling and technically detailed,
but much of the submission foregrounds team hardship and infrastructure. It
does not present a measured focus or life outcome, which weakens judge-
observable Peace evidence.

### 12. Rubric mapping

| Historical criterion | Heartbeat Hero evidence | Counterevidence | Analyst score | Control score | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Impact | Specific life-saving skill loop; free core; 100+ students reported | No independent learning or rescue outcome | 5 | 3 | High/medium |
| Feasibility | Working offline app, demos, accessible modes, no special hardware | Sensor accuracy and clinical validation not disclosed | 4 | 3 | Medium |

### What the evidence supports

Heartbeat Hero turns a broad social goal into observable practice and feedback.
LockedIn shows founder conviction and large technical scope without equivalent
proof that the product caused its intended benefit.

### What remains unknown

Independent depth validation, age/device/surface performance, knowledge
retention, real-emergency safety review, conversion, and long-term partnerships
remain unknown. LockedIn's adoption and safeguarding remain unknown.

### What may transfer to 2026

For high-stakes impact, demonstrate the smallest behavior the product can
change today, state what it cannot replace, and design a validation plan before
expanding the mission.

## Hearing Buddy

**Winner and match.** Hearing Buddy placed second in the 2025 Peace Prize.
CareWatch is the control: both are 2025 native SwiftUI assistive apps spanning
iPhone and Apple Watch, rely on new iOS 26 frameworks, emphasize private local
processing, and promise inclusion or care in time-sensitive situations.
CareWatch explicitly entered its case for the Peace Prize.

### 1. Track interpretation

Hearing Buddy defines impact as participation in ordinary group conversation.
Its free product provides unlimited on-device live captions; Pro adds speaker
detection and cross-device viewing. This makes both benefit and feasibility
observable during a single conversation.

CareWatch defines impact as better sleep and safer assistance by waking the
right family caregiver. The mechanism is similarly concrete, but routing based
on sleep/activity state and reliable escalation was less complete in the event
artifact.

### 2. Product thesis

Hearing Buddy prioritizes people who are hard of hearing in classrooms,
workplaces, and social groups. The trigger is missing speech or speaker context.
Workarounds include asking a “hearing buddy,” cloud transcription with hourly
limits, or leaving the conversation. Its wedge is unlimited offline Apple
Speech captioning, then trained speaker labels and question alerts on Watch.
The founder insight came from his partner's lived experience.

CareWatch prioritizes families caring at night for a person with chronic or
mobility needs. The trigger is a request for help while caregivers sleep. Calls
or texts can be missed or wake everyone. Its wedge is AlarmKit routing to the
on-call or most-awake person, with schedules and future escalation.

### 3. Core loop

Hearing Buddy: conversation starts → tap captioning → read live transcript →
label or teach familiar speakers → receive speaker/question cues → optionally
stream to Watch or Mac → remain engaged. Value arrives immediately and repeats
whenever conversation is difficult.

CareWatch: dependent requests help → system selects a caregiver → alarm/haptic
fires → caregiver responds → family reviews coverage and rebalances schedule.
Pro statistics monetize the reflection layer, not the urgent alert.

### 4. Journey

Hearing Buddy's demo presents free captioning first, then trains three speakers
in roughly five minutes, assigns a segment, toggles teacher mode, changes text
size, streams to Mac/Watch, and detects a question with a wrist tap. Onboarding
lets users name the buddy. Permission denial, unrecognized speech, noisy rooms,
language selection, restore, and transcript deletion are not fully shown.

CareWatch offers Help entry points through iPhone, Watch, widgets, live
activities, and optional on-device microphone rules. It pairs family members,
sets on-call schedules, chooses alarm strength, and plans escalation. The page
does not establish what happens on network loss, wrong routing, no response, or
dead battery—the most consequential recovery paths.

### 5. UI and interaction

Hearing Buddy's event art uses a bright blue caption card, friendly dolphin,
four-mode segmented control, and central Tap to Start. In the demo, caption text
dominates; speaker-training controls can be hidden for a minimal mode. Predicted
attention: Tap to Start → live caption region → speaker labels/Teach → mode
control. Text resizing and Watch haptics address different access needs.

CareWatch's inspected hero is a dark blue presentation card with tags, a huge
“Your family wants to stay at home” statement, and family portraits. It proves
audience and emotion, not the working alert interface. The underlying product
claims a big Need Help control and quick Watch/widget entry, but thumb reach and
alert acknowledgement cannot be visually verified.

### 6. Visual system

Hearing Buddy uses saturated blue, white, black, yellow caption emphasis, a
rounded dolphin character, grids, and star motifs. The brand is friendly while
the transcript surface remains high contrast. Promotional typography is dense
and mixes several display treatments, but the in-app mode can be minimal.

CareWatch uses navy, white, sky blue, pill tags, real family photography, and
large sans-serif type. The system feels credible and adult, although actual
screen-level accessibility evidence is sparse.

### 7. UX quality

Hearing Buddy's local speech, Bluetooth streaming, text size control, offline
operation, and free unlimited captioning are strong trust choices. Speaker
diarization is explicitly work in progress; mislabeling and missed questions
could confuse. Always-listening expectations, recording indicators, transcript
storage, bystander consent, and language/noise accuracy need explicit treatment.

CareWatch states that core features avoid always-on microphone and camera, and
that optional mic rules are opt-in and on device. It is an assistive tool, not
a medical device. Sleep-state inference, alert fatigue, wrong-person routing,
HealthKit sensitivity, and false negatives are material safety risks. The
planned fallback escalation is not yet evidence of a shipped recovery path.

### 8. Monetization

Hearing Buddy keeps unlimited real-time captions free. Pro adds speaker
detection and streaming to Mac/Watch, a strong value-aligned boundary that does
not remove basic participation. Exact price and trial are unknown.

CareWatch keeps alerting central and gates week-over-week coverage, response
time, and fairness statistics behind Pro. This is also aligned: urgent help is
not described as paywalled. Exact price and purchase journey are unknown.

### 9. Growth and retention

Hearing Buddy's launch in the hard-of-hearing subreddit received dozens of
positive comments, and beta testers continue to inform the product. Repeated
conversation need creates utility retention. No installs, active use,
caption-hours, or paid conversion are reported.

CareWatch reports in-home use by the builder's family but no sample size beyond
that household, alert success rate, sleep improvement, or outside adoption.
Family pairing can naturally invite another user, while reports may retain
caregivers.

### 10. Technical and operational shape

Hearing Buddy uses SwiftUI, iOS 26 Apple Speech, CoreML diarization, Bluetooth,
Watch and Mac apps, and RevenueCat. Moving from WhisperKit to Apple Speech
reportedly improved accuracy, performance, and battery while reducing vendor
cost. iOS 26 exclusivity narrows device reach; model updates, Bluetooth
reliability, and speaker enrollment remain risks.

CareWatch uses SwiftUI, Watch, AlarmKit, ActivityKit, WidgetKit, HealthKit,
Supabase, SQL/TypeScript, and RevenueCat. It has more remote coordination seams
than Hearing Buddy. Alarm delivery, server state, pairing, schedules, and
escalation need end-to-end reliability testing.

### 11. Submission craft

Hearing Buddy's demo starts with free, real-time captions, then proves the paid
differentiators with a three-speaker clip, live labeling, Mac/Watch streaming,
and question haptic. The founder story and public Reddit response reinforce the
need. Judges can see the mechanism, even though accuracy is not measured.

CareWatch's submission clearly explains who asks, who wakes, and why one-person
routing matters. Its screenshots emphasize story and routine integration, but
the submitted evidence does not visibly stress-test the critical alarm and
escalation paths.

### 12. Rubric mapping

| Historical criterion | Hearing Buddy evidence | Counterevidence | Analyst score | Control score | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Impact | Immediate conversational inclusion; unlimited free captions; lived-experience feedback | No measured participation or accuracy outcome | 5 | 4 | High/medium |
| Feasibility | Shipped on-device captions, Mac/Watch streaming, speaker demo | iOS 26-only; diarization still improving | 4 | 3 | High/medium |

### What the evidence supports

Hearing Buddy demonstrates a usable benefit in the ordinary context where the
problem occurs. CareWatch's equally credible care need is paired with more
unresolved reliability and recovery dependencies.

### What remains unknown

Caption word-error rate, diarization accuracy, battery use, language/noise
coverage, transcript privacy, and adoption are unknown. CareWatch's actual
alert-success and sleep outcomes are unknown.

### What may transfer to 2026

Keep the essential accessibility value free when possible, and monetize
coordination or convenience. In the demo, test a hard case and a recovery—not
only the happy path.

## MoodHaven

**Winner and match.** MoodHaven placed third in the 2025 Peace Prize. PCOS
Polly is the control: both are 2025 iOS health trackers founded on lived
experience, collect longitudinal sensitive data, use RevenueCat, and aim to
improve conversations or self-management. MoodHaven is native Swift;
PCOS Polly uses Expo/React Native but shipped on iOS. The control has stronger
commercial evidence and higher AI risk.

### 1. Track interpretation

MoodHaven interprets impact as giving parents a longitudinal, multi-caregiver
record of a child's moods, sleep, medication, and serious incidents for better
professional conversations. The mechanism is documentation and pattern
visibility, not diagnosis. Its impact claim is specific but outcomes remain
unmeasured.

PCOS Polly interprets benefit as an all-in-one routine, symptom, cycle,
nutrition, AI scanning, and support system for people with PCOS. It demonstrates
real adoption and payment, but makes a larger set of health-advice claims and
does not document clinical review.

### 2. Product thesis

MoodHaven prioritizes parents and caregivers whose child has intense mood or
behavioral episodes and whose brief appointments lose context. Workarounds are
memory, notes, or disconnected caregiver accounts. The wedge is one shared,
attributed timeline that can reveal patterns around mood, sleep, medication,
and incidents. The founder's seven-year-old son is the direct insight source.

PCOS Polly prioritizes people newly managing PCOS and overwhelmed by research,
symptoms, lifestyle changes, and unhelpful care. Its wedge is personalized
Gemini-generated routine and meal analysis integrated with daily tracking. The
founder's diagnosis grounds the need but does not validate recommendations.

### 3. Core loop

MoodHaven: a mood, sleep period, medication, or incident occurs → caregiver
logs it → other caregivers contribute → timeline and insights reveal patterns
→ family brings organized context to a professional → continue tracking.
First value is an organized record; higher value requires time and consistent
input.

PCOS Polly: personalized onboarding → paywall → daily nutrition/habit/symptom
tracking or meal scan/chat → recommendation and flower/streak feedback → repeat.
The core loop mixes tracking with advice and places payment before ongoing use.

### 4. Journey

MoodHaven's inspected home shows week state, current mood, energy and
pleasantness, today's chart, sleep, incidents, a central add action, Journey,
Resources, and Insights. Multiple caregivers are attributed. Exportable reports
and professional sharing were future work, meaning the claimed clinician handoff
was incomplete at submission. Authentication, child profiles, consent, deletion,
purchase, and crisis response are not shown.

PCOS Polly personalizes symptoms and goals, immediately presents a RevenueCat
paywall, then offers routine, meal scanner, habit/supplement tracking, period and
symptom timeline, AI chat, and reminders. The inspected symptom picker has clear
descriptions and a disabled action until selection. Export, deletion, restore,
urgent-care escalation, and adverse-advice recovery are not shown.

### 5. UI and interaction

MoodHaven's dark home uses a weekly strip, a green mood orb, orange Energy,
blue Pleasantness, line chart, and large sleep/incident cards. Predicted
attention: current mood and Check-in → colored scales → today's chart → sleep
and incident cards → central add/navigation. Decision density is high: the same
screen combines four domains, a chart, cards, and five navigation actions.
Color and labels are paired, but the gray secondary type may be hard to read.

PCOS Polly's symptom picker uses a pale pink sheet, large white rounded rows,
brown text, pink Cancel/Edit, and a full-width disabled Log Symptoms button.
Predicted attention: title → symptom rows → bottom action. Descriptions reduce
medical-term ambiguity, and the bottom action is reachable. The long list can
create fatigue and needs search/grouping for larger catalogs.

### 6. Visual system

MoodHaven combines near-black backgrounds, blue/green/orange signals, soft
glows, serif emotional labels, sans-serif data, thin line charts, and layered
rounded cards. It feels serious and polished, but the data dashboard risks
turning a child's experience into a dense monitoring surface.

PCOS Polly uses warm blush, cream, dark brown, pink actions, friendly flower
character art, and generous cards. It feels approachable. The soft contrast and
large feature set need accessibility verification.

### 7. UX quality

MoodHaven supports attribution and shared context, valuable for conflicting
caregiver recollections. It also stores highly sensitive child mental-health,
medication, sleep, hallucination, and violence data. Role permissions, child
assent, encryption, deletion, audit history, mandated-reporting boundaries,
crisis language, and clinician interpretation are not documented. Correlation
charts can be mistaken for causal or diagnostic evidence.

PCOS Polly handles sensitive reproductive, medication, nutrition, and symptom
data while using cloud AI and Firestore. AI meal ratings, supplement routines,
and chat can be mistaken for care advice. No clinician review, uncertainty
labels, contraindication checks, or crisis/escalation system is disclosed.

### 8. Monetization

MoodHaven uses a subscription, but event price, trial, free value, caregiver
seat model, and paywall timing are unknown. Charging for export or collaborative
records could impede the promised professional conversation; value-added
analytics or multi-caregiver coordination may be more defensible.

PCOS Polly places a RevenueCat paywall directly after onboarding and reports
strong conversion, 40+ payers, and $3,500 ARR by September 30. Packaging and
price are not shown. The early gate monetizes urgency before users can test the
quality of medical-adjacent advice.

### 9. Growth and retention

MoodHaven reports qualitative encouragement from families and community-driven
features, but no users, active families, logs, exports, or professional outcomes.
The longitudinal value and multi-caregiver contributions can retain families,
but also demand consistent work during stressful periods.

PCOS Polly reports 40,000 TikTok views, 300+ installs, 40+ paying customers,
and $3,500 ARR by the deadline; an October 7 update reports $11,100 ARR and must
remain post-deadline. Daily routines, reminders, a flower, streaks, and cycle
tracking provide multiple return triggers.

### 10. Technical and operational shape

MoodHaven uses Swift, Tuist, Clerk, Supabase, Novu, Rive, WeatherKit, and
RevenueCat. Multi-caregiver authorization, attribution, synchronization, and
sensitive-data security are the essential engineering risks. Novu and WeatherKit
add touchpoints whose role in the core loop is not explained.

PCOS Polly uses Expo/React Native, Firebase Auth/Firestore, Gemini, OneSignal,
RevenueCat, and several AI-generated features. This is operationally simpler
than MoodHaven's native/shared record in some respects, but model cost,
hallucination, privacy, and prompt safety are ongoing risks.

### 11. Submission craft

MoodHaven's founder story is powerful, and the event assets make an unusually
polished dashboard visible. The submission clearly lists the record types and
future clinician handoff. It does not show a complete report/export-to-
professional flow or outcome, so the most important impact bridge remains
described rather than observed.

PCOS Polly's screenshots demonstrate tracking, meal scan, advice, timeline,
flower, and chat, and the submission gives deadline-dated acquisition and
revenue. It proves commercial demand but not health benefit or safety.

### 12. Rubric mapping

| Historical criterion | MoodHaven evidence | Counterevidence | Analyst score | Control score | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Impact | Specific family documentation problem; shared longitudinal record | Professional handoff and care outcome not yet shown | 4 | 4 | Medium |

### What the evidence supports

MoodHaven addresses a real information-continuity failure with a coherent
recording mechanism. PCOS Polly proves that adoption, revenue, strong visuals,
and lived experience can coexist with substantial unaddressed safety risk.

### What remains unknown

MoodHaven's data governance, active use, caregiver agreement, clinician
acceptance, report quality, and effect on care are unknown. PCOS Polly's advice
quality, privacy, and health effects are unknown.

### What may transfer to 2026

For sensitive trackers, prototype the result-consuming workflow—not only data
entry. Show what a professional, caregiver, or user can safely decide from the
record, what they cannot infer, and how correction/deletion works.

## Batch contradictions and missing evidence

- Rakun's reported coffee and quiet-table success is not a usability study and
  does not cover varied communication disabilities.
- Heartbeat Hero's “millimeter” feedback is a builder claim without an
  independent sensor-validation protocol.
- Heartbeat Hero is a rebuilt public product based on a 2024 student prototype;
  it is not treated as wholly conceived during Shipaton 2025.
- Hearing Buddy's native Apple model was said to outperform prior WhisperKit in
  performance, accuracy, and battery, but comparative measurements are absent.
- CareWatch's future escalation and HealthKit routing cannot be counted as
  shipped reliability.
- MoodHaven's promise to improve professional conversations is not yet backed
  by the planned export/report flow or clinician observations.
- PCOS Polly's October 7 ARR is post-deadline and is kept separate from the
  September 30 snapshot.

## Batch recommendation

Carry forward “observable impact mechanism,” “risk-proportional trust and
recovery,” and “essential value remains accessible” as candidate patterns.
Do not yet conclude that founder adversity, health branding, platform-native
technology, or adoption differentiates winners; the controls also show those
features, sometimes with stronger commercial evidence.
