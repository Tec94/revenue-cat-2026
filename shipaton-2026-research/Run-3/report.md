# Run 3 report: cross-year winner pattern synthesis

The cross-year result is not a winning-feature checklist. Nearly every named
practice appears in both winners and plausible controls. The more defensible
signal is **evidence-chain completeness**: winners more often show a specific
problem, a shipped mechanism, an attributable product decision, and proof a
judge can inspect. This finding supports a proof-first build strategy for 2026,
but it does not reveal private judge reasoning or make any historical pattern
causal.

## Executive summary

This run tests nine user-selected patterns and eight prompt-mandated patterns
across all 39 officially placed 2024–2025 projects and the 16 unique matched
controls from Run 2. It uses one project as one instance and records weak,
strong, absent, and unknown evidence separately.

- No binary pattern clears a robust winner-differentiator test. Ten-second
  promises, short time to value, platform leverage, founder insight, visible
  polish, and judge-ready submissions also occur frequently in controls.
- The strongest quality gap is a complete feedback-to-result chain: strong
  support appears in 29 of 39 winners, or 74.4%, versus 4 of 16 controls, or
  25.0%. Simple evidence that “feedback happened” is much less distinctive.
- Judge-observable proof exists nominally in every winner and every control,
  but strong proof appears in 37 of 39 winners, or 94.9%, versus 11 of 16
  controls, or 68.8%.
- Monetization-after-value has a similar quality gap, 53.1% strong among
  assessed winners versus 26.7% among assessed controls, but eight projects
  are untestable. This is medium-confidence, not a universal conclusion.
- Measurable traction is not universal: it appears in 18 of 39 winners, or
  46.2%. It becomes decisive only where the historical or 2026 award wording
  asks for growth, virality, payment volume, or learning from a launch.
- Strong affirmative trust or accessibility evidence remains rare, including
  among winners. That is an opportunity under the 2026 Peace, OneSignal,
  Design, and selected influencer criteria, not proof that historical judges
  consistently rewarded it.
- The recommended 2026 posture is a narrow, immediately demonstrable value
  loop; one shipped differentiating mechanism; early external testing; an
  instrumented feedback-to-change chain; and category-specific proof visible
  in the first two minutes.

## Structured artifact and method

The full coding is available in the
[pattern codebook](./pattern-codebook.md),
[project-by-pattern recurrence matrix](./recurrence-matrix.csv), and
[calculated statistics](./pattern-statistics.csv). The nine selected
tests are P01, P02, P03, P04, P05, P06, P07, P10, and P11. S01–S08 are the
additional checks required by the Run 3 prompt. N01 is a newly observed
portfolio-level signal.

The coding scale prevents absence of evidence from becoming evidence of
absence:

- `2` is strong observed or traceable contemporaneous support.
- `1` is partial or weaker builder-described support.
- `0` is absence or counterevidence where assessment is possible.
- `U` is insufficient public evidence.

“Present” includes `1` and `2`; “strong” includes only `2`. Unknowns are
removed from the assessed denominator. The control set is purposive and small,
and a control's entered categories are often not public. Percentage gaps are
descriptive, not inferential statistics.

### Corpus coverage

The corpus is complete for official placements, but evidence depth is uneven.
All projects have a contemporaneous Devpost record. Eighteen winners and all
16 controls were examined in full Run 2 dossiers. The remaining 21 winners
received an independent full-text review for Run 3 before any comparison with
the earlier report.

| Cohort | Projects | Years | Evidence basis |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Official winners | 39 | 9 in 2024; 30 in 2025 | Official placement plus contemporaneous submission; multi-source dossiers for 18 |
| Matched controls | 16 unique | 4 in 2024; 12 in 2025 | Same-year public submissions; platform, mechanism, problem, or family match |
| Total coded | 55 | 2024–2025 | One row per project; 17 pre-defined tests |

Exa was the primary discovery and full-text extraction system. Firecrawl and
vision were used in Run 2 where dynamic assets or event-period screens needed
recovery. No new fallback was needed in Run 3 because this run measures
project-level recurrence rather than reconstructing additional interfaces.

## High-confidence transferable patterns

These patterns transfer because they recur with strong evidence, align with
observable judging mechanics, and map directly to 2026 criteria. Some are
minimum bars rather than winner differentiators.

### Make the proof chain judge-visible

P06 is the clearest transfer. Strong judge-observable proof occurs in 94.9% of
winners in both years and across every historical family. Controls also submit
proof, but it is less often complete, well sequenced, or tied to the target
rubric. The likely mechanism is practical: prescreeners can understand the
promise, see the build work, and verify award fit without private access.

The counterexample is a polished submission whose claims outrun the working
flow. Posturely's prototype/store-language conflict and projects with planned
rather than shipped functionality show that presentation cannot replace
eligibility or execution. This pattern is high-confidence for 2026 because the
official rules require a short demo and permit judging solely from the written
submission, images, and video.

### Close the feedback-to-result chain

P04 is present in 92.3% of winners and 81.2% of controls, so “we listened to
feedback” is not distinctive. The complete form is: observation or feedback,
decision, shipped change, and observed result. Strong support is 74.4% among
winners versus 25.0% among controls. It appears in both years and is strongest
in `#BuildInPublic`, Buzziest Launch, Best Vibes, and the 2025 Grand Prize.

The plausible mechanism is criterion coverage: a closed chain simultaneously
shows judgment, execution, learning, and momentum. Food Sense's navigation
change, MemoLune's rebuilds, Kigaru Talks' learner-segment correction, and
Friendy+'s weak-tie pivot are different products but the same evidence shape.
The alternative explanation is evidence availability: winners may document
their process more thoroughly after selection. Confidence is still high for
transfer because the 2026 `#BuildInPublic`, Grand Prize, Layers, and Idea to
Income wording explicitly rewards learning, iteration, or growth momentum.

### Ship one legible mechanism inside a coherent loop

P01 and P03 occur in every winner, but they also occur in 93.8% of controls.
They are therefore not binary differentiators. Strong execution is more common
among winners: 82.1% for loop coherence and 66.7% for a differentiating
mechanism. Information restraint, P05, is supportive rather than independent;
it has only a +6.4-point strong gap.

The transferable mechanism is compression. A judge can see one action produce
one promised result, while a shipped interaction or technical mechanism makes
the entry memorable. Shutter Declutter's date-constrained photo cleanup,
Napkinmatic's doodle-to-3D transformation, and Steps Share's friend-linked step
loop illustrate the structure. Apol, Dripped, Studient, and Posturely show the
counter-risk: a memorable idea can be diluted by broad scope, unclear trust
boundaries, or a pre-value gate. Confidence is high as product strategy, but
medium as a historical differentiator.

### Treat instant comprehension as the entry ticket

S01 is present in every coded project and P02 is present in every winner and
control. A ten-second promise and short first session are not winning secrets;
they are submission hygiene. The transferable lesson is to preserve them as
constraints while seeking differentiation elsewhere.

The counterexamples are products whose long-term value genuinely requires
history, relationships, or practice. Those products can still show an early
proxy value, preview, or useful setup result. Confidence is high that 2026
prescreening rewards immediate comprehension, but the corpus rejects these
traits as winner differentiators.

## Common practices that do not distinguish winners

The controls falsify several attractive heuristics. These practices remain
useful, but their mere presence should not drive concept selection.

- **A short time to value is not an edge.** P02 is 100% in both cohorts, and
  its strong rate is slightly lower among winners.
- **A clear elevator pitch is not an edge.** S01 is present in every project.
- **Founder insight is not sufficient.** S02 is present in every project. The
  specificity and decision relevance of the story are better among winners,
  but a personal anecdote does not prove demand.
- **Platform APIs do not confer differentiation by themselves.** S04 is 100%
  present and about 69% strong in both cohorts.
- **A visual signature is common.** S07 is present in every project. Its craft
  is stronger among winners, but that effect is partly category selection and
  evidence quality.
- **Early testing is common.** P11 differs by only 3.4 percentage points at the
  presence threshold. What matters is whether the runway produces an
  attributable change or metric.
- **A distribution feature is not automatically viral.** S06 appears at nearly
  the same rate in winners and controls. Recipient value, observability, and
  conversion are the missing parts.

## Failure modes, evidence gaps, and misleading heuristics

The corpus supports rejection of the following shortcuts. These are research
warnings, not claims that every named project failed overall.

- **Counting features instead of mechanisms.** A broad submission can score a
  `1` on many patterns while providing less strong evidence than a narrow app.
- **Equating public posting with iteration.** Frequency, views, and follower
  count do not prove a feedback-to-result chain.
- **Treating traction as universally required.** More than half of the winners
  lacked a meaningful event-period traction metric. The criterion matters most
  in growth-oriented awards.
- **Treating founder pain as market validation.** Personal insight explains
  problem selection; it does not establish frequency, severity, willingness
  to pay, or distribution.
- **Assuming polish explains selection.** Strong controls such as Monk,
  Stupido, Mood Dial, and Restia exhibit many winner-like practices.
- **Using current store presentation as historical evidence.** Current assets
  are excluded unless explicitly labeled. Restia's later gallery changes are a
  known timing limitation.
- **Using a sensitive-domain promise without proportional safeguards.** PCOS
  Polly, ReadHim, MoodHaven, and Dripped illustrate different trust, clinical,
  relationship, or bias risks. The matrix does not validate their substantive
  claims.
- **Reading an analyst code as a judge score.** No score in the matrix is an
  actual Shipaton score, and no private deliberation was observed.
- **Award accumulation.** The official rules do not impose a general
  non-influencer entry cap, but the operating contract still limits a concept
  to one primary and at most two coherent secondary non-influencer awards, plus
  no more than one Influencer Award.

## Newly observed pattern: evidence-chain density

N01 emerged after the pre-defined tests were coded. Winners average 58.8% of
assessed patterns at strong support, compared with 44.1% for controls. The
median is 58.8% versus 50.0%. Steps Share, Shutter Declutter, Meshing, Karo,
Dayloop, and Hearing Buddy are high-density examples; Monk is a counterexample
control with winner-level density.

The plausible mechanism is not “do more things.” Strong rows repeatedly link
specificity, working product behavior, a decision trail, and observable proof.
This lets one artifact satisfy several criteria without bolt-on features. The
alternative explanations are substantial: selected winners may have better
submission records, the control set is small and purposive, and later organizer
attention can improve source discoverability. N01 is therefore medium
confidence and newly observed, not a causal winner formula.

## Changes from 2024 to 2025

The historical corpus changed from three broad families and nine placements in
2024 to a larger, sponsor-shaped set of 30 placements in 2025. The expansion
changes which patterns recur, so raw cross-year increases are not necessarily
changes in judge preference.

| Change | Observed effect in the corpus | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Design and `#BuildInPublic` retained their original criteria | Visual craft and feedback chains recur in both years | These are the cleanest historical comparisons |
| 2024 business viability split into 2025 Grand, launch, and HAMM concerns | Strong traction rises from 0/9 winners in 2024 to 11/30 in 2025 | Category composition and better metric reporting both explain the increase |
| 2025 added Peace, OneSignal, KMP, Best Vibes, and sponsor/staff awards | Trust, platform leverage, retention, and visual signatures appear in new contexts | Do not infer a universal rubric shift from sponsor-specific placements |
| Early experiment runway strengthened in 2025 | Strong P11 rises from 22.2% to 40.0% | Consistent with launch/growth awards, but presence remains similar |
| Strong founder insight increased | 55.6% in 2024 versus 76.7% in 2025 | Better storytelling or source richness is an alternative explanation |
| Platform-native leverage stayed stable | 66.7% strong in 2024 and 70.0% in 2025 | It is a general implementation practice, not a new universal differentiator |

The 2024 “Most Likely to Make Money” award is only partially comparable to
2026. It directly scored business viability, design/execution, monetization,
onboarding/paywall design, and ASO. The 2025 Grand Prize instead scored Early
and Effective Release and Growth by numbers. The 2026 Grand Prize keeps those
criteria but adds a material RevenueCat-reported event-period revenue
shortlist. HAMM isolates revenue-model quality, while launch and growth are
distributed among Grand, Noise, Layers, Idea to Income, and Stripe.

## Opportunity territories

These are research territories, not product concepts. Each is framed around a
recurring decision or workflow and a primary evidence mechanism. A later run
must identify all plausible users, select a priority segment, compare its pain
with the general user base, and validate frequency, severity, workarounds, and
willingness to change.

### T1 — Private help in routine but consequential moments

Investigate non-regulated situations where a user must act under time,
attention, confidence, language, sensory, or social constraints. The territory
tests whether immediate guidance can produce a safe first-session result while
keeping permissions, uncertainty, and recovery proportional to the stakes.
Natural primary-award orientations are Peace or Design, but only after the
audience and benefit mechanism are validated.

### T2 — Capture-to-usable-artifact workflows for solo work

Investigate fragmented tasks where a creator, student, field worker, or solo
professional repeatedly captures messy input and must turn it into a decision,
record, handoff, or publishable artifact. The territory has a naturally
judge-visible transformation, short time to value, and a testable
monetization-after-value boundary. Design or HAMM may become a primary award;
Grand requires actual early revenue and growth evidence.

### T3 — Small-group utility with recipient-side value

Investigate coordination, accountability, sharing, or progress problems where
each invited person receives independent value rather than becoming a growth
target. This territory can test an authentic distribution loop, measurable
activation, and feedback-driven iteration without requiring a mass social
network. Noise, Layers, or Grand could be primary orientations depending on
which mechanism proves central.

### T4 — Adaptive cross-device field workflows

Investigate tasks that break when users move between phone, tablet, desktop,
foldable, split-screen, or weak-connectivity contexts. The opportunity is not
generic cross-platform availability; it is continuity and form-factor-specific
value. Ship Kotlin Everywhere or Best App for Galaxy could become a primary
orientation. This territory carries the highest native-integration and test
matrix cost for a solo builder.

### T5 — Respectful return loops for intermittent goals

Investigate useful behaviors that recur irregularly and are poorly served by
streak pressure or generic reminders. The territory tests whether state-aware
re-engagement, deep-linked next actions, and recovery from lapses create value
without coercion. Keep Them Coming Back or Layers may become a primary award,
provided instrumentation and message delivery remain architecturally simple.

## Contradictions and missing evidence

These limitations are material to the synthesis and remain unresolved.

- The control cohort is only 16 projects, is purposively matched, and is not a
  random sample of all non-winners. Most control category selections are not
  public.
- The cohorts are unbalanced: nine winners in 2024 and 30 in 2025. Several
  award families contain only one or three projects, so family percentages can
  swing sharply.
- P07 is untestable for eight projects because event-period monetization timing
  or placement is not visible. It should not be generalized at high
  confidence.
- Public evidence rarely shows complete empty, loading, error, cancellation,
  offline, and recovery paths. Strong trust codes are therefore conservative.
- Builder metrics use different dates and definitions. They are not pooled as
  a common performance measure or independently audited.
- Napkinmatic AI3D contains platform and store-timing tension; Studient contains
  build-date tension; Posturely uses prototype language that conflicts with the
  general live-store requirement; One Step's 2024 match is inferred; and some
  Restia imagery is post-event.
- The rules and overview historically contain timing or eligibility wording
  conflicts. The user's instruction controls planning: August 1 is treated as
  the official start, and the age-of-majority rule controls over broad overview
  copy.
- No analytics, eye tracking, click tracking, usability study, private judge
  notes, or actual category scores were obtained. Predicted attention findings
  from Run 2 remain hypotheses, not heatmaps.

## Recommendation

Proceed to audience and pain-point research with **T2, T3, and T4** as the
balanced default set. T2 tests a compact solo-buildable value transformation
and coherent payment boundary. T3 tests the strongest underexploited growth
mechanism—recipient-side value—without assuming a mass network. T4 tests the
most explicit sponsor/platform opportunity, while exposing native complexity
early enough to reject it if the solo-builder cost is excessive.

This is a recommendation, not a product or award decision. T1 is a strong
substitute if impact is strategically preferred, but it requires tighter trust
and domain boundaries. T5 is the lower-integration substitute if T4's device
matrix proves too expensive.

## Decision gate

Select exactly three territories for the next audience and pain-point research
run:

- **Option A — Balanced default:** T2 + T3 + T4.
- **Option B — Trust and impact:** T1 + T2 + T5.
- **Option C — Growth and retention:** T2 + T3 + T5.
- **Custom:** any three of T1–T5, or request one new territory derived from a
  named pattern or 2026 criterion.

Do not select a concept or primary award at this gate. The next run should
compare plausible user groups and prioritize problems before solution design.

## Next steps

After the gate, the next run should build an audience map for each selected
territory, identify triggering situations and current workarounds, compare
general and priority segments, and rank pains by frequency, severity, urgency,
ability to reach users, and feasibility for a solo builder. Concept generation
should remain closed until those problem rankings are accepted.
